# Bottom line

COVID-19 patients with myocarditis or pericarditis had higher 6-month mortality (OR 1.36 and 2.55) and more new cardiovascular sequelae than matched controls. Incident heart failure was an outcome, but the abstract gives no heart-failure estimate.

- **Design** — Retrospective cohort study using electronic medical records from a global federated network, with 1:1 propensity score matching
- **N** — 718,365
- **Population** — Patients with COVID-19, with and without new-onset myocarditis or pericarditis
- **Outcome** — 6-month all-cause mortality, hospitalisation, cardiac arrest, incident heart failure, incident atrial fibrillation and acute myocardial infarction; heart failure criteria not given in the abstract
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.77)

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. Six-month all-cause mortality was 3.9% (n = 702) in patients with myocarditis and 2.9% (n = 523) in matched controls (p < .0001), odds ratio 1.36 (95% confidence interval (CI): 1.21-1.53).
2. Six-month all-cause mortality was 15.5% (n = 816) for pericarditis and 6.7% (n = 356) in matched controls (p < .0001), odds ratio 2.55 (95% CI: 2.24-2.91).
3. Patients with pericarditis seemed to associate with more new-onset cardiovascular sequelae than those with myocarditis.

# Caveats

Only the abstract was read, so heart-failure results are missing. Diagnoses come from electronic records and may be misclassified, and matching does not remove residual confounding. Severity, critical care and detection bias may affect the associations.
