# Bottom line

VPC diagnosis independently predicts incident systolic HF (adjusted HR 1.8), with the strongest effect in younger patients lacking comorbidities (HR 6.5). VPCs may be a cause of 'idiopathic' HF.

- **Design** — Population-based retrospective cohort using California HCUP hospitalization database 2005-2009, multivariable Cox models with interaction analyses
- **Population** — California hospitalized patients 2005-2009, 35,817 with VPC diagnosis
- **Outcome** — Incident systolic heart failure identified from hospital discharge diagnoses in HCUP database
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — moderate (0.56) — nothing to score on size

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. After adjusting for potential confounders, VPCs were associated with a nearly twofold risk of systolic HF (HR 1.8, 95% CI 1.8 to 1.9, p <0.001).
2. A VPC diagnosis in younger patients (<65 years) without coronary artery disease, hypertension, diabetes, or atrial fibrillation exhibited a sixfold increased risk of systolic HF (HR 6.5, 95% CI 5.5 to 7.7, p <0.001).
3. Incidence of systolic HF was 62.8 per 1,000 patient-years (95% confidence interval [CI] 61.2 to 64.4) in those with and 6.1 per 1,000 patient-years (95% CI 6.1 to 6.2) in those without VPCs (p <0.001).
