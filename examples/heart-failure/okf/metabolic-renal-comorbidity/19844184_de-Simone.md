# Bottom line

Type 2 diabetes was an independent predictor of incident heart failure (fully adjusted HR 2.45; 1.50 when incident MI was censored as a competing event). Impaired fasting glucose carried no excess risk versus normal glucose.

- **Design** — Population-based prospective cohort (Strong Heart Study), Cox regression with MI as competing risk event
- **N** — 2,740
- **Population** — American Indian adults aged 45-74 without prevalent CVD or severe kidney disease, 3 US regions
- **Outcome** — Incident HF adjudicated by committee using major/minor clinical criteria (2 major or 1 major plus 2 minor), mean follow-up 11.9 years
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.75)

# Vocabulary hints

- **type 2 diabetes (fasting glucose >=7 mmol/L or treatment)**
- **impaired fasting glucose (6–6.9 mmol/L)**
- **GFR by simplified MDRD**
- **central fat distribution (NCEP-ATPIII waist cutoffs)**

# Caveats

No echocardiography at baseline, so cardiac phenotype could not be assessed; silent ischemia cannot be excluded. Censoring HF after MI assumes MI caused it; single baseline measurements in American Indians limit generalizability.
