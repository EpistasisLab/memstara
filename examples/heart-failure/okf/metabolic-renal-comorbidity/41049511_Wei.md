# Bottom line

Cumulative MetS burden was dose-dependently associated with incident HF (adjusted HR 2.62 for 5 components vs 0). Raised blood pressure conferred the greatest risk, while raised triglycerides showed an inverse association after adjustment.

- **Design** — Population-based prospective cohort study (UK Biobank), Cox proportional hazards models
- **N** — 388,827
- **Population** — UK Biobank adults aged 40-69 without baseline HF, median follow-up 13.2 years
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure, ICD-10 I50, from UK Biobank First occurrence field (self-report, primary care, hospital, death registry)
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.93)

# Caveats

Non-fasting blood samples were used for glucose and triglycerides, which may explain the inverse triglyceride finding. Single baseline measurement, observational, and the UK Biobank is a healthy-volunteer cohort; 111,124 were excluded for missing MetS data. Adjusting for BMI alongside waist circumference and for other components may distort component estimates.
