# Bottom line

Rurality was independently associated with incident HF (adjusted HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.13-1.26) after adjusting for CV risk factors, behaviours and SES. The association varied by race and sex, greatest in Black men and null in White men.

- **Design** — Prospective cohort study (Southern Community Cohort Study linked to CMS claims)
- **Population** — Predominantly low-income Black and White adults in the southeastern US without HF at enrollment, CMS-linked
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure from CMS diagnosis codes through 2016
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — moderate (0.67) — nothing to score on size

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. After adjustment for demographic information, CV risk factors, health behaviors, and SES, rural participants had a 19% greater risk of incident HF (hazard ratio [HR], 1.19; 95% CI, 1.13-1.26) compared with their urban counterparts.
2. The rurality-associated risk of HF varied across race and sex and was greatest among Black men (HR, 1.34; 95% CI, 1.19-1.51), followed by White women (HR, 1.22; 95% CI, 1.07-1.39) and Black women (HR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.08-1.28).
