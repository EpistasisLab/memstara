# Bottom line

Weaker social relationships (combined score 1) were associated with higher incident heart failure after full adjustment (HR 1.38, 1.02–1.87). Marital status and living alone were not significantly associated, and single-item contact measures lost significance after full adjustment.

- **Design** — Prospective population-based cohort (British Regional Heart Study), Cox proportional hazards models
- **N** — 3,698
- **Population** — Men aged 60–79 years from 24 British towns, free of prevalent heart failure or MI at 1998–2000
- **Outcome** — Incident non-fatal heart failure (GP/hospital record, doctor-confirmed and verified) or death from heart failure (ICD-9 428/ICD-10 I28)
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.81)

# Caveats

Men only, all older and from British towns, so generalisability is limited. Social measures are self-reported at a single time point, and the fully adjusted estimates are borderline with attenuation after adjusting for health status, which may be mediators. The paper reports 330 events in the abstract but 338 in Table 1.
