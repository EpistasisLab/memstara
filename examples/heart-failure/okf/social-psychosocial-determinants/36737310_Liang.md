# Bottom line

Social isolation and loneliness each raised incident HF risk by about 17-19% after full adjustment, independent of polygenic risk. Loneliness modified the isolation association (Pinteraction 0.034).

- **Design** — Population-based prospective cohort study (UK Biobank) with Cox models
- **N** — 464,773
- **Population** — Community-dwelling UK adults aged 40-69 enrolled 2006-2010, free of HF at baseline
- **Outcome** — Incident HF from hospital admission or death records (ICD-10 I11.0, I13.0, I13.2, I50), follow-up to May 2021
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.93)

# Caveats

Isolation and loneliness measured once at baseline with brief self-report items; UK Biobank is healthier and less deprived than the general population. Residual confounding possible, and HF ascertained from hospital/death records only. Genetic analyses restricted to White participants.
