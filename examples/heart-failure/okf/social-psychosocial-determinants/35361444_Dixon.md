# Bottom line

Greater depressive symptoms were modestly but significantly associated with incident HF after full adjustment (HR 1.04 per 8 CESD-10 points), without variation by race or sex. The association was present only among those not taking antidepressants.

- **Design** — Prospective cohort (Southern Community Cohort Study) with CMS claims linkage for HF
- **N** — 23,937
- **Population** — Black or White adults aged 40-79, low-income, southeastern US, CMS-covered, without prevalent HF, MI or CABG
- **Outcome** — First CMS claim with ICD-9 428.x or ICD-10 I50 or I110 through Dec 31 2016; censored at HF, death or end of follow-up
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.86)

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. With further adjustment for socioeconomic and behavioral factors, social support and anti-depressant/anxiolytic use, higher CESD-10 score remained significantly associated with increased risk for incident HF (HR 1.04, 95% CI: 1.00–1.09; p = 0.038); Table 2, Supplemental Table 2, and Supplemental Figure 2. Central Illustration illustrates the predicted probability of incident HF at 5 years according to CESD-10 score.
2. In an age-, sex-, and race-adjusted model, each 8 point higher CESD-10, corresponding to the difference between the 25th to 75th percentiles of CESD-10 scores, significantly associated with increased risk for incident HF (HR 1.17, 95% CI: 1.13–1.21; p <0.001).
3. The association between CESD-10 and HF risk was attenuated but still significant after adjustment for traditional clinical HF risk factors (HR 1.11, 95% CI: 1.07–1.15; p <0.001).
4. In another sensitivity analysis, we examined whether a diagnosis of depression, defined as a composite of self-reported history of depression or anti-depressant/anxiolytic medication use, associates with incident HF, with similar findings (HR 1.06, 95% CI:1.00–1.13, p = 0.045).
5. Figure 1 demonstrates CESD-10 score associated with increased HF risk among participants without anti-depressant/anxiolytic use (HR 1.07, 95% CI: 1.02–1.12; p = 0.006), but not among participants using these medications (HR 1.00, 95% CI: 0.94–1.07; p = 0.91).

# Caveats

Depressive symptoms measured once at baseline; HF ascertained from claims, with possible misclassification. Fully adjusted effect is small with CI touching 1.00. Restricted to CMS-covered Black and White participants, and residual confounding is possible.
