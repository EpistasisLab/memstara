# Bottom line

Incident HF after MI was common and exceeded recurrent MI risk, even in patients with preserved EF and no HF at discharge. Incident HF was associated with higher death and rehospitalization irrespective of EF.

- **Design** — Retrospective multi-hospital electronic health record cohort with 3-month landmark analysis
- **N** — 6,804
- **Population** — Adults with primary discharge diagnosis of MI at 28 Baylor Scott & White hospitals, 2015-2021
- **Outcome** — Incident HF: first HF diagnosis within 3 months after discharge, or HF diagnosed during index admission with a subsequent HF diagnosis within 3 months; EF measured at HF diagnosis or within 6 months.
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — moderate (0.51)

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. Patients who developed incident HF had a higher risk of death (HR 1.76, 95% CI 1.49–2.07; Figure 3A).
2. A higher proportion of STEMI vs. NSTEMI (26.1% vs. 23.0%, P = .026) patients developed incident HF.
3. Compared with patients who did not develop HF, those who did had a higher risk of death (8.1% vs. 3.2%) and all-cause (31.2% vs. 19.0%) and CV rehospitalization (14.2% vs. 6.7%) (all P < .001; Table 3).
4. Among 2179 patients with LVEF ≥ 50% at discharge from index hospitalization who did not have a prior history of HF and did not die or develop HF during index MI hospitalization, 257 (11.8%) developed incident HF and 77 (3.5%) developed recurrent MI in the 1 year following discharge.
5. Data for secondary analysis on all patients with full follow-up data available are shown in Supplementary data online, Table S4: we observed substantial differences, with older age (median 71 vs. 67 years) and higher prevalence of T2D (49% vs. 40%), CKD (36% vs. 21%), atrial fibrillation/flutter (22% vs. 16%), and peripheral artery disease (22% vs. 13%) independently among patients who developed incident HF.
6. Serum N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) and serum brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) measured during index acute MI hospitalization were higher among patients with incident HF including those with LVEF ≥ 50% compared with those who did not develop incident HF (see Supplementary data online, Table S3).

# Caveats

Retrospective EHR data with HF ascertainment by coding and possible post-MI stunning; mortality HRs appear unadjusted, and no adjusted predictors of incident HF were modelled. Loss to follow-up outside the health system is possible, and HF includes cases diagnosed during the index admission.
