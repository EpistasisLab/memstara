# Bottom line

Isolated diastolic hypertension was associated with higher incident HF (adjusted HR 4.65, 95% CI 1.09-19.90) and a non-significant trend to higher CV mortality. Based on only 5 HF events in the exposed group.

- **Design** — Prospective population-based cohort (Cardiovascular Health Study), secondary analysis of public-use data, 13 years follow-up
- **N** — 524
- **Population** — Community-dwelling US adults aged 65+ with isolated diastolic hypertension (n=27) or normal BP (n=497)
- **Outcome** — Incident HF over ~13 years, centrally adjudicated from self-reported physician-diagnosed HF using medical records, HF medication treatment and supporting evidence
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.76)

# Caveats

Only 27 exposed participants and 5 HF events give very wide CIs and possible chance findings. The comparison group was a selected ideal normal-BP group, and residual confounding is possible. Exposed participants differed at baseline (more African American, diabetes, higher BMI), and half were treated.
