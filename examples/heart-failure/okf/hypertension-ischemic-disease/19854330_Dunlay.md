# Bottom line

Coronary disease and hypertension each accounted for about 20% of incident heart failure; coronary disease carried the highest odds (3.05) and hypertension a weaker odds (1.44) but high prevalence. Five risk factors together explained 52% of cases.

- **Design** — Population-based case-control study, incident HF cases matched to controls by age and sex, conditional logistic regression
- **Population** — Olmsted County, Minnesota residents with incident heart failure 1979-2002 and age/sex-matched controls (mean case age 75.4, 53.7% women)
- **Outcome** — First diagnosis of heart failure (ICD-9 428), validated by Framingham criteria and physician diagnosis; index date at first evidence in medical record
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.70) — nothing to score on size

# Caveats

Case-control with risk factors from medical record review, possible under-recognition. Mainly Caucasian population. Risk factors overlap and lie on causal pathways, so individual PARs are not additive; valvular and renal causes not examined.
