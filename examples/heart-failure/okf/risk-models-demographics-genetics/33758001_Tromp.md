# Bottom line

Traditional risk factors had stronger relative risk and greater population attributable risk for incident HF in younger than older adults, and model discrimination was better in the young (C index 0.79 vs 0.64). Absolute risk was higher in older people.

- **Design** — Pooled population based prospective cohort study (FHS, PREVEND, MESA) with age-stratified Cox models
- **Population** — Community-dwelling adults without prior heart failure, stratified by age (<55, 55-64, 65-74, ≥75 years)
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure adjudicated by experts from signs/symptoms and records within 15 years; subtyped HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%), HFrEF (<50%) or unclassified
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.81) — nothing to score on size

# Caveats

Observational pooled cohorts with harmonized baseline risk factors; participants with missing covariates excluded (n=1063). Risk factors measured once at baseline and HF adjudication differed across cohorts; older groups had competing risk of death and higher unclassified HF. Genetics and family history not examined.
