# Bottom line

Hypertension (PAF 25.8-28.3%) led in Black adults and obesity (17.9-21.0%) in White adults; diabetes contributed most in Black women (16.4%). All five factors together explained 39.3% (White women) to 51.9% (Black women) of incident HF.

- **Design** — Pooled analysis of six prospective population-based cohorts (Lifetime Risk Pooling Project) with competing-risk PAF estimation
- **N** — 38,028
- **Population** — Black and White US adults aged <=80 free of clinical CVD at baseline
- **Outcome** — Incident HF adjudicated per cohort-specific pre-specified criteria; non-HF death treated as competing risk
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.87)

4. We estimated the population attributable fraction (PAF), which incorporates both prevalence and excess risk of HF associated with each risk factor (obesity, hypertension, diabetes, current smoking, and hyperlipidemia), in specific race-sex groups.

# Caveats

Only baseline risk factors, with no time-varying exposure; only Black and White participants, with cohorts of differing eras. Race-sex PAF differences mostly lack formal tests, and CIs are wide for obesity in Black adults. HF adjudication varied by cohort.
