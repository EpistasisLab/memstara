# Bottom line

RA was associated with a 30% higher fully adjusted rate of first HF hospitalization versus the general Danish population. The association held in new-onset RA, both sexes, and with TNF inhibitor adjustment.

- **Design** — Nationwide register-based cohort study (prevalent RA cohort, Poisson regression), with inception-cohort sensitivity analysis
- **Population** — Danish adults aged ≥18 years without prior HF, 2008-2012; 24,343 with rheumatologist-diagnosed RA vs 4,280,882 reference
- **Outcome** — First hospital admission for HF (primary or secondary diagnosis; ICD-10 I42, I50, I110, J819)
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.73) — nothing to score on size

1. Correspondingly, the fully adjusted incidence rate ratio for incident HF was increased in patients with RA with incidence rate ratio 1.30 (95% confidence interval, 1.17–1.45).
2. The time‐dependent multivariable Poisson regression analyses, adjusted for age, sex, and calendar year, showed increased risk of HF in patients with RA compared with the reference population with IRR 1.64 (CI, 1.53–1.77).
3. Correspondingly, the fully adjusted IRRs for incident HF were 1.44 (CI, 1.26–1.65) in patients with RA in this analysis.
4. When we excluded all subjects, who were registered with HF as a secondary diagnosis and only focused on subjects with HF as a primary diagnosis (27 303 cases), the association between RA and HF was not considerably altered (IRR, 1.43 [CI, 1.25–1.65]).
5. When the primary study end point was modified to include a prescription for loop diuretics and a hospital diagnosis of HF, the RA‐associated increased risk of HF remained statistically significant with IRR 1.30 (CI, 1.26–1.35) in the fully adjusted analysis.

7. Concerning the impact of sex on risk of HF in patients with RA, we found a nominally slightly increased IRR (1.34 [CI, 1.18–1.53]) for females compared with males (IRR, 1.22 [CI, 1.02–1.47]), but there was no statistical significant difference between the 2 groups.

# Caveats

Register-coded HF is under-reported (sensitivity 30-50%), and smoking and alcohol were proxied by drug/diagnosis codes, so residual confounding is likely. Prevalent RA design has survivor bias, and no RA severity, disease activity or serological data were used. Outcome is HF hospitalization, not chronic HF incidence per se.
