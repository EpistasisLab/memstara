# Bottom line

RA was associated with a 21% higher adjusted risk of incident HF (HR 1.21, 95% CI 1.03-1.42). Within RA, higher CRP increased HF risk (OR 1.29) and methotrexate was associated with lower risk (OR 0.75).

- **Design** — Retrospective EHR cohort (matched RA vs non-autoimmune controls) plus a small prospective proteomics/cardiac MRI cohort
- **Population** — Adults with rheumatoid arthritis and age-, sex-, race-matched controls without autoimmune disease at Vanderbilt University Medical Center; no prevalent HF
- **Outcome** — Incident HF: ≥1 ICD-9 code 425.x or 428.x plus intravenous diuretic use within 90 days of the code
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.73) — nothing to score on size

1. In multivariable‐adjusted models, RA was associated with a 21% (95% CI, 3–42%; P=0.023) increased risk of HF.
2. In the subset of patients with RA in whom ESR and CRP levels were measured (n=6161, Table S2), greater levels of inflammation as measured by CRP were associated with increased risk of HF (OR, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.16–1.44 [P<0.001]).
3. The use of methotrexate was significantly associated with lower risk of HF (odds ratio [OR], 0.75; 95% CI, 0.59–0.96 [P=0.021]).
4. Compared with patients with RA who did not develop HF, those who did were older at RA diagnosis, with a higher frequency of coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, traditional cardiovascular risk factors, and chronic kidney disease (Table 5).
5. Atrial fibrillation was the comorbidity most strongly associated with the risk of HFrEF, accounting for 11% of the model and an OR of 4.62 (95% CI, 2.38–8.97; P<0.001), although it was not associated with HFpEF (P=0.27).
6. Methotrexate use was associated with lower risk for HFpEF (OR, 0.64; 95% CI, 0.55–0.98 [P=0.036]) but not HFrEF (P=0.68), while corticosteroids were associated with lower risk for HFrEF (OR, 0.53; 95% CI, 0.34–0.81 [P=0.004]) but not HFpEF (P=0.98).

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. ESR trended toward but was not significantly associated with the risk of HF when CRP was also included in the model (OR, 1.13; 95% CI, 0.98–1.30 [P=0.097]).
2. Atrial fibrillation was the comorbidity most strongly associated with the risk of HFrEF, accounting for 11% of the model and an OR of 4.62 (95% CI, 2.38–8.97; P<0.001), although it was not associated with HFpEF (P=0.27).
3. LVEF near the time of HF diagnosis was available in 79% of patients with HF and was similar between patients with (median, 55%; 25th–75th percentile: 40–60%) and without RA (median, 55%; 25th–75th percentile: 35–60% [P=0.44]).
4. Antirheumatic medications were variably associated with the risk of HF.

# Caveats

EHR-based with ICD-defined outcomes; differing follow-up between RA and controls and possible confounding by indication for medications. Medication analyses used logistic regression among RA patients with ever-use exposure. The proteomics/MRI cohort is small (n=115) and cross-sectional.
