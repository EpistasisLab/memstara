# Bottom line

Long-term PM2.5 and its constituents were associated with higher incident HF, with elemental carbon contributing most (43%). PM2.5 constituents and genetic risk acted synergistically, accounting for 9-16% of HF events.

- **Design** — Prospective cohort (UK Biobank) with Cox models, quantile-based g-computation and additive interaction analysis
- **Population** — UK Biobank adults, median follow-up 12.05 years, 7554 incident HF cases
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure during follow-up (definition not detailed in abstract)
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.71) — nothing to score on size

# Caveats

Abstract only; covariates and HF ascertainment not described. Modelled exposure at residential address may be misclassified, and the UK Biobank is a healthier-than-average volunteer cohort. Constituents are highly correlated, so individual effects are hard to separate.
