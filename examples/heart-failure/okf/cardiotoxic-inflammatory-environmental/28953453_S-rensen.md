# Bottom line

Long-term residential road traffic noise and NO2 exposure were each associated with higher incident heart failure risk, mainly in men, and remained so (weaker) with mutual adjustment. Highest risk was with high exposure to both.

- **Design** — Prospective cohort (Danish Diet, Cancer and Health) with registry linkage, Cox models with age as time scale
- **N** — 50,935
- **Population** — Danes aged 50-64 at enrollment (1993-97) in Copenhagen/Aarhus areas, no prior cancer or heart failure
- **Outcome** — First-ever hospital admission for heart failure in the Danish National Patient Register (ICD-8 427.0/427.1; ICD-10 I50, I11.0, I42.0, I42.9), follow-up after 1 July 1997 to 2011
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.88)

# Caveats

Modeled residential exposure with no data on noise barriers, indoor/workplace exposure or PM; residual confounding possible and noise/NO2 correlated (r=0.65). Lifestyle covariates measured only at baseline; 8% excluded for missing data; hospital-diagnosed HF only.
