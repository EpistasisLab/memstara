# Bottom line

HIV infection was associated with a nearly two-fold risk of incident heart failure (RR 1.80, 95% CI 1.51-2.15) with high heterogeneity. The association was nominally stronger in younger adults, women and those with CD4<200.

- **Design** — Systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies (MOOSE, PROSPERO registered)
- **N** — 8,848,569
- **Population** — Adults free of HF at baseline, people living with HIV vs HIV-negative controls, from 8 cohorts (US, Taiwan, France, UK)
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure ascertained mainly by ICD-9/ICD-10 diagnostic codes
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (1.00)

Other demographic or clinical characteristics such as age, race/ethnicity (in particular the proportion of self-identified black participants), cardiovascular risk factors (proportion reporting hypertension, diabetes, or dyslipidemia), and baseline percentage of participants with established cardiovascular disease did not appear to modify the association between HIV and HF incidence.
2. Similarly, in meta-regression analyses, we found evidence to suggest that the association between HIV and HF risk decreases as the proportion of men in the study increase, though this did not reach statistical significance (β=−1.33; P=0.24).

# Caveats

High heterogeneity (I2 84.7%) and few studies per subgroup; subgroup differences mostly nonsignificant. Observational, with varying adjustment and code-based ascertainment; one OR used as RR; publication bias suggested; ART effects not assessed.
