# Bottom line

Elevated copeptin independently predicted incident heart failure (HR 1.30 per SD) after conventional risk factors, eGFR and MRproANP. The association was no longer significant for the MI-related and non-MI-related subgroups after MRproANP adjustment.

- **Design** — Population-based prospective cohort (Malmö Preventive Project re-examination) with register-based outcome follow-up
- **N** — 5,297
- **Population** — Swedish adults aged 53-81 without prevalent HF, re-examined 2002-2006, 69.6% men
- **Outcome** — Incident HF from national inpatient, outpatient and cause-of-death registers (primary diagnosis, ICD-9 428 / ICD-10 I11.1, I50), split into MI-related and non-MI-related HF
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.82)

1. The hazard ratio (HR) per SD increase in log‐transformed copeptin for incident HF was 1.30 (95% CI: 1.17–1.46).
2. Individuals free from HF at baseline and belonging to the top quartile of copeptin had, after multivariate adjustment for conventional risk factors, a significantly increased risk of developing HF by 63% for any HF, by 100% for MI‐related HF and by 47% for non‐MI‐related HF, compared with the reference quartile 1 (Table 2 ).
3. Individuals free from HF at baseline and belonging to the top quartile of copeptin had, after multivariate adjustment for conventional risk factors, a significantly increased risk of developing HF by 63% for any HF, by 100% for MI‐related HF and by 47% for non‐MI‐related HF, compared with the reference quartile 1 (Table 2 ).
4. Individuals free from HF at baseline and belonging to the top quartile of copeptin had, after multivariate adjustment for conventional risk factors, a significantly increased risk of developing HF by 63% for any HF, by 100% for MI‐related HF and by 47% for non‐MI‐related HF, compared with the reference quartile 1 (Table 2 ).
5. The associations remained after additional adjustment for estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) [HR 1.24 (95% CI: 1.10–1.40)].
6. Furthermore, the association between copeptin and HF remained significant after adjustment for log‐transformed MRproANP on top of conventional risk factors [HR 1.14 (95% CI: 1.02–1.28)].
7. When analysing the association between copeptin and the two HF subgroups, the risk of non‐MI‐related HF per SD increase in log‐transformed copeptin was significant after adjustment for conventional risk factors [HR 1.26 (95% CI 1.11–1.44)], remained significant when additionally adjusted for eGFR [HR 1.20 (95% CI 1.04–1.38)] but became non‐significant when adjusted for MRproANP on top of conventional risk factors [HR 1.12 (95% CI 0.98–1.28)].
8. When analysing the association between copeptin and the two HF subgroups, the risk of non‐MI‐related HF per SD increase in log‐transformed copeptin was significant after adjustment for conventional risk factors [HR 1.26 (95% CI 1.11–1.44)], remained significant when additionally adjusted for eGFR [HR 1.20 (95% CI 1.04–1.38)] but became non‐significant when adjusted for MRproANP on top of conventional risk factors [HR 1.12 (95% CI 0.98–1.28)].
9. Finally, the risk of HF per SD increase in log‐transformed copeptin was significant in both men [HR 1.28 (95% CI 1.13–1.46)] and women [HR 1.40 (95% CI 1.11–1.77)] after adjustment for conventional risk factors.

# Caveats

Copeptin measured once in a random subsample; NTproBNP and CRP were not available for adjustment. HF identified only via primary register diagnoses, likely missing cases. Subgroup MI-related findings are hypothesis-generating; a single Swedish cohort limits generalizability.
