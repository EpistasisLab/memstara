# Bottom line

A small panel of circulating proteins predicted incident HF and its subtypes (C-index 0.78-0.80) and carried the risk information of clinical data, performing best for events within 1 year. Findings replicated in the Cardiovascular Health Study.

- **Design** — Prospective population-based cohort (AGES-RS) with LASSO regression and bootstrap protein selection; external replication in CHS
- **Population** — Older adults in the AGES-Reykjavik Study, with replication in the Cardiovascular Health Study
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure after first visit, including HFrEF and HFpEF subtypes; median follow-up 5.45 years
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.71) — nothing to score on size

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. A least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression model with non-parametric bootstrap were used to select predictors from an analysis of 4782 serum proteins, and several pre-established clinical parameters linked to HF.
2. A subset of 8-10 distinct or overlapping serum proteins predicted different future HF outcomes, and C-statistics were used to assess discrimination, revealing proteins combined with a C-index of 0.80 for all incident HF, 0.78 and 0.80 for incident HFpEF or HFrEF, respectively.
3. A subset of 8-10 distinct or overlapping serum proteins predicted different future HF outcomes, and C-statistics were used to assess discrimination, revealing proteins combined with a C-index of 0.80 for all incident HF, 0.78 and 0.80 for incident HFpEF or HFrEF, respectively.
4. In the AGES-RS, protein panels alone encompassed the risk contained in the clinical information and improved the performance characteristics of prediction models based on N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide and clinical risk factors.
