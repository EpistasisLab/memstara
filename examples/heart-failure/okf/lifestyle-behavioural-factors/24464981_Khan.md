# Bottom line

Higher cardiorespiratory fitness was independently and inversely associated with incident heart failure in a dose-response pattern. Adding CRF to established risk factors did not significantly improve discrimination (C-index change 0.0164, P=0.07).

- **Design** — Prospective population-based cohort (Kuopio Ischaemic Heart Disease study type), mean follow-up 20.4 years
- **N** — 1,873
- **Population** — Finnish men aged 42-61 years without heart failure or chronic respiratory disease at baseline
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure events (152 recorded during mean 20.4 years follow-up)
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.70)

1. The age-adjusted hazard ratio (HR) per unit increase (1 mL/kg/min of VO2 max) in CRF was 0.89 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.86-0.93], which was minimally attenuated to 0.94 (95% CI 0.90-0.98) after further adjustment for established HF risk factors (body mass index, systolic blood pressure, history of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, heart rate, and LV hypertrophy) and incident coronary events as a time-varying covariate.
2. The age-adjusted hazard ratio (HR) per unit increase (1 mL/kg/min of VO2 max) in CRF was 0.89 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.86-0.93], which was minimally attenuated to 0.94 (95% CI 0.90-0.98) after further adjustment for established HF risk factors (body mass index, systolic blood pressure, history of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, heart rate, and LV hypertrophy) and incident coronary events as a time-varying covariate.
3. In a comparison of extreme quartiles of CRF levels (VO2 max ≥35.4 vs. ≤25.7 mL/kg/min), the corresponding HRs were 0.27 (0.15-0.50) and 0.48 (0.25-0.92), respectively.
4. Each 1 MET (metabolic equivalent of oxygen consumption) increment in CRF was associated with a 21% (7-33%) reduction in multivariable adjusted risk of HF.

# Caveats

Men only, Finnish, so generalizability is limited. Abstract only; the age-adjusted quartile HR (0.27) is ambiguous in labelling. Few events (152) and residual confounding by physical activity or other behaviours are possible.
