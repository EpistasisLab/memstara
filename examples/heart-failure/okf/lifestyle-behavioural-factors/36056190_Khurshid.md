# Bottom line

Accelerometer-measured MVPA at or above 150 min/week was associated with 35% lower incident heart failure risk after adjustment, a stronger effect than for self-reported MVPA (HR 0.84). Greater MVPA was associated with lower risk across most of 697 diseases.

- **Design** — Prospective cohort (UK Biobank), Cox proportional hazards models, phenome-wide association with FDR 1%
- **N** — 96,244
- **Population** — UK Biobank adults wearing a wrist accelerometer for one week (mean age 62); comparison sample of 456,374 with self-reported activity
- **Outcome** — Incident disease (including heart failure) from hospital and general practitioner records; median follow-up about 6.2-6.3 years
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.90)

# Caveats

Observational with residual confounding possible (healthy-user effects); one-week accelerometer wear and short median follow-up (~6 years) with possible reverse causation. Heart failure was defined from hospital/GP records and the sample is mostly White, healthier UK Biobank volunteers.
