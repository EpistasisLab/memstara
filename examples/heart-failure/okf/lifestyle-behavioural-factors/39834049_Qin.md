# Bottom line

Low-to-moderate total alcohol intake was associated with ~13-21% lower incident HF risk vs never drinkers (J-shaped), with maximum benefit at 14 units/week in men and 7 in women. Beer intake was associated with higher HF risk in women (p sex interaction=0.002).

- **Design** — Population-based prospective cohort (UK Biobank), analysed retrospectively; Fine-Gray competing-risk models and restricted cubic splines
- **Population** — Middle-aged UK Biobank participants (52% women, mean age 56) without baseline HF
- **Outcome** — Incident HF from hospital inpatient records (ICD-10 I50, I110, I130, I132, Z941, T862; ICD-9 428; OPCS4 K02) and self-report; death as competing risk

# Caveats

Single baseline self-reported alcohol measure; never drinkers may include former drinkers (sick-quitter bias) and had highest HF risk. Residual confounding likely, and few women drank beer heavily, giving wide CIs. HF includes self-report and inpatient records; UK Biobank is a healthy-volunteer cohort.
