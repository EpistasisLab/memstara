# Bottom line

Current smoking, higher intensity and burden independently predicted incident HF hospitalization in African Americans, even after accounting for incident CHD. Former smoking showed no significant association.

- **Design** — Prospective community-based cohort (Jackson Heart Study) with cross-sectional CMR and BNP analyses plus longitudinal Cox analysis
- **N** — 3,633
- **Population** — African American adults in Jackson, Mississippi without prior CHD or HF at baseline
- **Outcome** — Adjudicated HF hospitalization (ICD-9-CM primary diagnosis reviewed by adjudicators), surveillance 2005-2012; 147 events
- **Read from** — the full text
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.81)

2. In the current study, former smoking was not associated with adverse cardiac remodeling, impaired cardiac function, BNP levels or incident HF hospitalization after adjusting for possible confounding factors.

# Caveats

Only 147 HF events, with wide intervals; smoking self-reported and HF only hospitalizations. African American cohort only, and the CMR subset was small; former smoker null may reflect low power.
