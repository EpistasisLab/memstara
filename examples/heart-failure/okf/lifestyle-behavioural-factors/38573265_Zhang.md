# Bottom line

Each 3-point higher EAT-Lancet diet index was associated with 7% lower adjusted HF risk over 25 years. An eight-protein proteomic score mediated about 9.4% of the association.

- **Design** — Prospective population-based cohort with register-based HF ascertainment and a proteomics subcohort
- **N** — 23,260
- **Population** — Swedish population-based cohort adults (Malmö), 1,768 incident HF cases; proteomics subcohort n=4,742
- **Outcome** — Incident heart failure identified through the Swedish national register
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.77)

Quoted from the paper, by row:

1. After adjusting for sociodemographic, lifestyle, diabetes, hypertension, use of lipid-lowering drugs, and body mass index, the HR per 3-point increase of the EAT-Lancet diet index was 0.93 (95% CI: 0.88-0.97).
2. Among the included 136 plasma proteins, a total of 8 proteins (AM, GDF15, IL-6, TIM, CTSD, CCL20, FS, and FUR) were both inversely associated with the EAT-Lancet diet index and positively associated with risk of HF; the overall proteomic score mediated 9.4% (95% CI: 2.2%-32.1%) of the association.
