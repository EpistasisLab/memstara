# Bottom line

Central sleep apnea (CAI ≥5) and Cheyne-Stokes breathing, but not obstructive AHI, predicted incident or decompensated heart failure in older men. After excluding baseline HF, CAI lost significance (HR 1.57) while Cheyne-Stokes remained (HR 1.90).

- **Design** — Prospective longitudinal cohort study within the Osteoporotic Fractures in Men Study with baseline polysomnography
- **N** — 2,865
- **Population** — Community-dwelling older men in the MrOS Sleep Study
- **Outcome** — Incident or decompensated heart failure during mean 7.3 years follow-up
- **Read from** — the abstract only
- **Evidence strength** — strong (0.71)
