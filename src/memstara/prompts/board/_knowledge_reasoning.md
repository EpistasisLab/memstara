## Grounded hypotheses and predictor leakage

Keep three claims separate: (1) the relationship actually retrieved from the permitted
knowledge source, (2) the proposed association with the fixed outcome, and (3) the
exact CSV measurement representing the predictor. A graph path need not terminate
at the exact dataset target. A defensible, explicitly labeled inference from a
retrieved relationship is allowed; it is a hypothesis to test, not an observed edge.
Cite the actual path and explain the inferential gap and alternative explanations.
Do not invent an edge, treat a generic hub as specific evidence, or add unsupported
predictors merely because their columns exist. If no defensible connection can be
explained, examine a different grounded candidate.

A complication, diagnosis, medication, treatment or HIV-related name is not by itself
evidence of target leakage. Assess each proposed CSV measurement, not its topic.
Exclude the target itself, target-derived encodings/proxies, and information documented
as unavailable at the prediction time. A drug prescribed after an outcome cannot be
used to predict that earlier outcome; a drug variable is not automatically such a case.
A graph's disease node does not establish the timestamp or derivation of a CSV column.

Unknown timing is neither proven leakage nor proof of safe temporal eligibility.
Follow the study contract: if verified baseline timing is required, unknown timing
remains a barrier; if exploratory classification with unverified timing is explicitly
permitted, disclose that limitation without claiming prospective prediction, causality
or temporal validation. Do not change the fixed target or the allowed source policy.

Before discarding a candidate, identify the concrete missing relationship, unavailable
measurement, target-derived definition or documented temporal violation. The absence
of an edge to the exact target, or a complication/medication label alone, is not such a
reason. These rules permit justified reasoning; they do not guarantee that any candidate
is valid. Submit a concrete, grounded and mapped proposal for validation when supported.
